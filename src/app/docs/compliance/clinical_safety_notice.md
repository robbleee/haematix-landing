# Clinical Safety and Intended Use Notice

**Last updated:** 7 July 2026  
**Version:** 1.0

Haem.io is clinical decision support software for healthcare professional use.

It is designed to support haematology and myeloid disease classification workflows by applying deterministic, explainable logic aligned to published classification and risk frameworks. It is not intended to replace clinical judgement.

## 1. Intended Users

Haem.io is intended for use by appropriately qualified healthcare professionals, clinical scientists, researchers, and authorised institutional users.

It is not intended for direct patient use.

## 2. Intended Use

Haem.io supports:

- structured review of haematology, molecular, morphology, and related clinical data;
- deterministic classification support using published criteria;
- explainable classification traces and audit records;
- clinical workflow support for multidisciplinary discussion, research, validation, and service improvement.

## 3. Not a Medical Device Notice

Unless a specific deployment or product version is expressly stated otherwise in a signed agreement or regulatory documentation, Haem.io is not currently certified, registered, or approved as a UKCA, CE, FDA, or other regulated medical device.

We are working toward appropriate clinical safety, validation, and regulatory pathways, but users must not treat the current public website or non-certified deployments as an autonomous diagnostic device.

## 4. Clinician-in-the-Loop Requirement

Outputs must be reviewed by a suitably qualified professional before being used in clinical decision-making.

Users remain responsible for:

- verifying input data accuracy and completeness;
- confirming that the case falls within the tool's intended scope;
- reviewing all assumptions, warnings, and derivation traces;
- considering local guidelines, patient context, and multidisciplinary judgement;
- documenting final clinical decisions in the appropriate clinical record.

## 5. Limits of the Tool

Haem.io may produce incorrect, incomplete, or inappropriate outputs if:

- source data is missing, inaccurate, ambiguous, or entered into the wrong field;
- the disease context falls outside the supported scope;
- guidelines have changed and the deployment has not yet been updated;
- local practice differs from the guideline implementation;
- the user enters identifiable or irrelevant information into a public tool;
- classification criteria require judgement or confirmation that cannot be inferred from structured data alone.

## 6. Public Website Tools

Public website tools are intended for demonstration, education, exploration, and non-identifiable structured use. Do not upload or paste identifiable patient information into the public website.

For clinical service use, use an approved customer deployment with appropriate governance, DPIA, access controls, and local clinical safety approval.

## 7. On-Premise Deployments

On-premise deployments may be configured for identifiable clinical data under the customer's governance. The customer is responsible for local clinical safety case approval, user training, access control, incident reporting, and recordkeeping unless otherwise agreed.

## 8. Reporting Issues

Report suspected safety issues, incorrect outputs, security concerns, or privacy incidents to [robert.lee@haem.io](mailto:robert.lee@haem.io).

Please do not include identifiable patient information in ordinary email unless it is necessary, authorised, and sent through an appropriate secure channel.
